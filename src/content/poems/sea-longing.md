*In response to Garry Fabian Miller’s ‘The Sea Horizon’* 

  


Forty seas static in a room, quiet motionless

and yet I bring in something with me.

Inchoate, restless, rearing at the very 

walls of the gallery. The echoes do 

not escape my frame, layers of clothing

absorb the sound, cloaking me in 

rushing movement. 

  


A thin presence pressing outward

from the brume. A dark shadow:

Coastline cast clearest into focus

by a cut of light, solid in the 

colour’s blend. The detail is 

haze, but the faint city, knife-thin

on the horizon, promises me. 

  


Another, an inky, textured expanse

looms below, and up from it,

mist rises into a trunk of 

blue cloud. Distant golden light

catches on every fold of water.

I hover from frame to frame, my 

Eyes each time, snapping to the centre.

  


Wales flits in and out of view, 

clear one moment, over brown

estuary water and vanishes the next

into the implication of a dramatic 

roiling sky. Eighty eyes look out

across the waters, looking for 

something. 

  
  


Alone in the room, I shift my 

weight, one foot after the other,

letting the creaking floor cover

the blood surging behind my ears.

Restless, I keep still before each 

and every sea. Bury the urge to 

turn the camera, to look back. 

  


I cannot stay long or I’ll fall

away into the brume. Inside at least

I’m fixed in place, but leaving 

into daylight, the sound washes out

leaving me empty and Zugunruhe, 

migratory panic, wells up and slowly

quells as waves crest and roll 

  


over passing cars.
