There in the crowded rumble of
the central line we stand. Your 
hair, close shorn and dyed bright.
I catch myself trying to recognise you.

You don’t clock me, most 
people don’t, but like a brush of
cool air in the tunnel heat the 
sight of you eases me.

I see successive pale waves
lap at your wrist as you hold
the handrail. How could you
be a stranger to me, when 

you look so like every face of my youth.
