Waxier paper improves the crunch 
when I snatch them from the 
slot on my way out.

I’ve made a habit of taking the others,
left behind by prior shoppers.
The flotsam of their week, like 
necromancy comes to my call and
tells me their stories.
Dashing out for some paprika, milk, an onion.
A dozen date nights, snack runs, last minute 
recollections, all sung out for me.

When I’ve a place of my own,
I think I’ll stick them to my wall, overlapped
so all the edges are lost in one another. 
When I sleep, I’ll look at my pale blue wall of songs,
and dream of waves crashing and rolling
for no one in particular.
