title: Haunt
date: 2025-01-29

I met you at your hotel that morning,
you were effervescent, copper-brown twill
beaming in the light. Hovering, I fret
at my dress and makeup, it’s my first
public appearance as a woman and you,
deft-with-compliments, assure me with
bristled kiss. I kept close to you before
the ceremony, enjoying our newly altered 
connection, let you revel in your role:
Proud father of a graduating daughter.
As I walked across the stage, I looked 
back to see if I could catch you from 
the crowd, but you were lost in shadow
behind all the stage lights. 


After we’d said our goodbyes, we bumped 
into each other again, outside the station.
We said our second farewells mirthfully, 
another parting hug, snatched from chance
and fate, and parted ways again, looking 
forward to our holiday next month. 
“We’ll see you again in a month or so”
It felt different, more grown-up somehow. 
I missed you by just a week in the end,
and would not see you alive again.
I have you outside the station, warm 
orange in evening light, which was not so,
and hold you longer than I would have. 
I cannot remember without inventing.

To your funeral, I went in suit, and set
aside my last filial duties, laying down 
manhood with your coffin, on the dais.
