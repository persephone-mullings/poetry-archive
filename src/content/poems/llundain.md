I took a kind of comfort in rainfall,
the pulling of coat tighter, even tensing 
shoulders that will later ache. The train
came in as I did, the boxier cars that
only call at later hours. I caught no
glimpse of the scant few passengers, disembarking
into heavy rain and instead sequestered 
myself into the grey dim light. The high 
idle whir is second nature to me, as is 
the clumsy rolling crash of transit. When 
echoed, it awakens something, underground,
perhaps ancestral: my great-grandfather
digging out tunnels under rock and clay,
far below the washing reach of the river,
come out east to England for work with rock.
Almost unnoticed, the mechanical 
workings kick in, closing doors and 
humming into motion and gears
within me slow and I settle 
into my empty carriage. 
We made our journeys parallel, he and I, 
meeting in the middle, decades apart.
When you arrived, it was a new home.
Mine seems gone as the door 
slides to a thud closed behind me.
