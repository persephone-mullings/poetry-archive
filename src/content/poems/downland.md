Up on the downland, the bone breaks 
chalky through the brittle earthen skin. 
Rends appear, here and there, in which
you may see deep into the heaving rolls 
of time. The chalk, even muddy and 
worn, is stark white cutting through 
the gloom. The woods are haunted and 
remember the sea that stretched from 
here east, across the continent. 
Bones of ancient sea life fell and 
were folded atop one another, 
compacting into the body of the earth. 

As a child I’d march always up the 
crunching rocky paths, rough underfoot
and treacherous even in early evening, up
to the woods. The chalk roads, ploughed
up by land rovers and tractors, sunk 
into the earth, the canopy arching over 
head, such that it formed a kind of 
tunnel. I’d imagine a great snake, 
a basilisk, dragging its immense 
body across the ground, carving 
holloways through the forest and
graving its mark across the landscape. 

Deep in my bones, the fire still
crackles and spits in the woodburner,
the chalk earth is crunching under 
foot and the winds still course over 
the valley. I saw it last in the winter,
the brume came in tides through the
forest and closed the world to a stone’s
throw around me. It gave way only to the
purple-amber soak of evening, which
cast the landscape in unreal colours.
An aurora hangs above the hills, pink and 
green waves sweeping over valleys. 

The blood rushes in my hearing, and 
the ghosts holding seashells to my ear
walk in crunching steps.
