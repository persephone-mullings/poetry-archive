Sat in our silent company,
I spare you my eyes,  
expectant-looking, on your 
calmed drifting face.

My eyes settle at your 
knees, folded under you and
unpick the weave of your 
jeans. Each black thread

catching the light, deepening 
the night sky conforming
to your shape. Set across 
your form, I pick out 

a hundred lint stars, trace 
constellations around the 
curve of your leg. In the slight shifts
of your breathing. I trace up 

the curve of your thigh
and each star now blends
into white-grey cloud
swirling in the dark air.

You’re almost cast in golden
streetlight. Me trudging home
the dark alive with possibility,
the cold air is breathy, keen.

My half-self, is nocturnal 
still, she shines in evenings,
candle-like when I lay my
head down in your lap.
