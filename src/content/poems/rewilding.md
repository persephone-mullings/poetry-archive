title: Rewilding

I spent a lifetime
ripping up weeds, sending the
shreds to the bonfire. I found
my choicest cuts and 
threw out the rest.
I made my life a lifeless place. 
Scaled down to perfect size
I sat in peace and quiet.
Prescient, I marked out my plot
and laid my grave modestly,
tastefully. 
I woke half buried, alone,
untouched, even evading carrion. 
No bulbs grew for foxes to hunt
all already dug up and what
was left of me? I traded all 
my grisle for smooth, clean bone. 
But one day, I chose to live. 
I let my land heal, 
took what was left of me,
let it mulch, and from it grew
thickets and flowers and brambles.
The plants brought mice and 
insects, feeding birds and foxes.
I clothed my bones in moss
and vines and pelts, filled out
my hollow shape again.
Before long there were guests,
strays and monsters mostly but 
they loved my bones and I 
kissed their fangs. 
We haunt, a danse macabre, 
jaunty in spite of ourselves.
I’ll be death,
you can be revival,
and between us we’ll remember
how to live.
