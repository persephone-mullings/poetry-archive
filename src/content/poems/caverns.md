When rain comes, the water 
seeps into the skin's cracks and
courses deep through the body
into every root and abscess.

Unrelenting, it settles in, a new
permanent fixture, it widens 
arteries, erodes space away and 
weighs the body to a steady indolence.

This heavy grinding thing, now 
second nature, sits in the 
chest, feeding and eroding.
It's patient and subtle as fear.

One day, somehow, it dries up. 
The course flows out and you didn't 
notice. When you move, the worn 
smooth caverns still howl in wind like waves.

You ring when struck, a high echoing
chime, for a moment fills empty air.
