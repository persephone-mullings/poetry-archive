They would have given them to me 
if I liked but, in my state,
freshly defanged, I hardly heard. 

The surgeon told me the roots 
of my teeth were fearsome  
long. I saw on the x-ray how 

they reach high up into my 
skull. In the end they split 
the tooth in three, ripping each

out individually, one by one. 
Forty-five minutes grew deep
into hours, settling in my bones. 

With the tooth gone, the infection
washed out with gravity, falling
from me as the pressure beneath

my skin gave out and was released.
I wish sometimes, I’d kept those 
shards. A trophy from myself. 

‘These bastard’s didn’t come out 
easy,’ I’d say, a dumb show, knocking
at my skull, ‘but she’s a tough one.’ 

But maybe its better to be rid
of them. The wound’s all healed
over, and my mouth almost fits
how it used to when I smile.
