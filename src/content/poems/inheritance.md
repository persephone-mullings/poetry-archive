A bird rises into the grey.
My eyes graze along
wingspan, colour, tail, but

I have nothing. Too big
for a falcon, I know,
they're small beasts.

Each time I find myself
wanting to walk the
miles and miles home,

find my father, building his
guitars in the kitchen, furrowed
dark look as he does,

And make him unfurl to me
all he can on bird and tree.
Hours on pinions and

Swooping. Ash trees are
tall and pale with purple
flowers. Elms are dark

with rough bark
and leaves. On my
shelves are books that

tell me these things
but they wash clean
from my mind.

A great brown flash
glides over the treeline,
Redkite, maybe.

I sit by the window
catching glimpses, silent
behind the music

of wind through trees
under wings.
